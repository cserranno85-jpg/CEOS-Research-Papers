# Contributing to CEOS Research Papers

CEOS research contributions should improve correctness, falsifiability, reproducibility, clarity, or evidentiary quality.

## Suitable contributions

- corrections with supporting evidence;
- literature or prior-art references;
- formalization improvements;
- reproducible experiments;
- adversarial test cases;
- benchmark designs;
- replication results;
- threat-model improvements;
- documentation that clarifies implemented versus proposed behavior.

## Research contribution requirements

A substantive contribution should identify:
1. the claim or research object affected;
2. the evidence or reasoning supporting the change;
3. whether the change concerns fact, implementation observation, hypothesis, formalization, or experimental result;
4. any known counterevidence or limitations;
5. reproducibility information where applicable.

## Not accepted as evidence by itself

- model confidence;
- unverified summaries;
- screenshots without provenance where stronger evidence is available;
- green CI unrelated to the claim being made;
- provider or agent assertions that are not independently grounded;
- citation count or popularity as a substitute for technical evidence.

## Publication changes

Do not silently modify the meaning of a released paper. Material changes should produce a new version or explicit correction record.
