# CEOS Research Integrity Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the minimum epistemic and publication standards for research artifacts in CEOS-Research-Papers.

## Core rules

1. **Observation is not inference.** Directly observed repository, experimental, or external-system facts must be distinguishable from analytical interpretation.
2. **Implemented is not deployed.** Source capability, merged code, successful CI, validation, runtime activation, deployment, and production operation are separate claims.
3. **Planned is not implemented.** Proposed architecture and theoretical mechanisms must be labeled accordingly.
4. **Evidence is not authority.** Evidence may support a decision but does not itself grant permission or establish canonicality.
5. **Model confidence is not proof.** Model-generated analysis is an analytical artifact unless independently established by applicable evidence.
6. **Negative results are results.** Failed hypotheses, contradictory evidence, benchmark losses, and inability to reproduce must not be suppressed merely because they weaken a preferred thesis.
7. **Falsification over confirmation.** Important claims should be accompanied by a plausible condition under which they would be considered false or weakened.
8. **Exact versions matter.** Repository observations should identify the repository, branch or release where appropriate, and exact revision when material.
9. **No silent historical rewriting.** Material corrections to released research should be recorded through a new version, erratum, or correction notice.
10. **External precedent must be acknowledged.** CEOS should not claim primitive novelty for established concepts such as least privilege, complete mediation, leases, provenance, policy engines, sagas, or causal ordering.

## Epistemic classes

- **Canonical CEOS fact**
- **Live repository observation**
- **External technical fact**
- **Analytical formalization / hypothesis**
- **Experimental result**, when a documented experiment has actually been performed

## Formal claims

A mathematical expression is not a proof merely because it is written formally. Papers must distinguish:
- descriptive notation;
- analytical formalization;
- proposed invariant;
- machine-checked property;
- mathematically proved theorem;
- empirical result.

## Corrections

A material correction should identify the affected paper/version, original claim, corrected claim, reason, and date. Released artifacts should remain recoverable.

## AI-assisted research

AI systems may assist with drafting, analysis, literature discovery, code, critique, and experimental design. Human maintainers remain responsible for publication decisions, source verification, claim classification, and correction of errors. AI assistance must not be represented as independent peer review.
